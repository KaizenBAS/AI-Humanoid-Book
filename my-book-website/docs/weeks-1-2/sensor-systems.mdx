# Sensor Systems: LIDAR, Cameras, IMUs, Force/Torque Sensors

## Introduction

Sensory information forms the foundation of physical AI systems. A robot's ability to perceive its environment is critical for safe and effective operation in physical space.

## Types of Sensors

### LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)
LiDAR sensors emit laser pulses and measure the time to return to create precise 3D maps of the environment.

**Applications:**
- 3D mapping of surroundings
- Obstacle detection and avoidance
- Navigation and localization (SLAM)
- Object recognition and distance estimation

**Advantages:**
- High precision distance measurements
- Works in various lighting conditions
- Can detect transparent and reflective surfaces

**Limitations:**
- Expensive compared to other options
- Can be affected by weather conditions
- Limited vertical resolution in some sensors

### Cameras
Cameras provide visual information in the form of images or video streams.

**Types:**
- **RGB Cameras**: Standard color imaging
- **Stereo Cameras**: Depth estimation using two lenses
- **Thermal Cameras**: Heat signature detection
- **Event Cameras**: Ultra-fast response to motion
- **Polarization Cameras**: Surface property detection

**Applications:**
- Object recognition and classification
- Visual odometry and SLAM
- Human detection and gesture recognition
- Surface property analysis

### IMUs (Inertial Measurement Units)
IMUs combine accelerometers, gyroscopes, and sometimes magnetometers to measure motion and orientation.

**Components:**
- **Accelerometer**: Measures linear acceleration
- **Gyroscope**: Measures angular velocity
- **Magnetometer**: Measures magnetic field (heading reference)

**Applications:**
- Robot stabilization and balance
- Orientation tracking
- Motion detection and classification
- Navigation in GPS-denied environments

### Force/Torque Sensors
Force/torque sensors measure applied forces and moments in multiple axes.

**Types:**
- Strain gauge-based sensors
- Piezoelectric sensors
- Optical sensors

**Applications:**
- Precision manipulation tasks
- Assembly operations
- Human-safe interaction
- Surface contact detection and characterization

## Sensor Integration

### Sensor Fusion
Multiple sensors are combined to improve perception:

- **Kalman Filters**: Combine uncertain measurements optimally
- **Particle Filters**: Handle non-linear and multi-modal distributions
- **Deep Learning**: End-to-end learning from multiple sensor inputs

### Calibration
Proper calibration is essential for accurate sensing:

- **Intrinsic Calibration**: Internal sensor parameters (e.g., camera focal length)
- **Extrinsic Calibration**: Relationship between sensors and robot frame
- **Temporal Calibration**: Synchronization of sensor readings

## Practical Considerations

### Noise and Filtering
Real sensors have noise that must be handled:
- Low-pass filtering for stable readings
- Robust statistics for outlier rejection
- Adaptive filtering for changing conditions

### Latency
Minimizing sensor-to-decision latency is critical:
- Real-time processing requirements
- Communication and computation delays
- Asynchronous sensor data handling

### Environmental Factors
Operating conditions affect sensor performance:
- Temperature variations
- Dust and moisture protection
- Vibration isolation
- Electromagnetic interference
